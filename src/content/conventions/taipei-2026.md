We attended the five-day Rotary International Convention from 13–18 June, among some 37,000 Rotarians from around the world — our 17th, and as inspiring as ever.

Our booth in the House of Friendship was colourful and well visited. Most new visitors had never heard of the Fellowship, so it was a wonderful chance to share our multidisciplinary, evidence-based approach, our openness to non-Rotarians, and our branches in Egypt, Uganda and Ukraine.

At our one-hour Fellowship meeting we heard four member presentations — mobile healthcare in Uganda (Mauri Okamoto-Kearney), tuberculosis prevention in Nepal (Leckman), affordable hernia repair through Hernia Aid (Andrew Kingsnorth), and lessons from Kangaroo Mother Care in Ukraine (John Philip) — followed by Ann Evans on preventable blindness.

Our supply of Kangaroo Mother Care carriers to neonatal units in Ukraine drew particular interest: a low-cost, high-impact project we are now preparing to strengthen and expand through a workshop in Moldova, with support already from clubs in RGBI, Canada and Austria.

Eight new members joined the Fellowship at the Convention.
